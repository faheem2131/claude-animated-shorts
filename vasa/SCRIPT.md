# The Warship That Sank Before Lunch: Vasa, 1628

## Facts (verify each against a primary/museum source before recording)
- Built 1626-28 in Stockholm for King Gustavus Adolphus; carried two gun decks, about 64 guns. [Wikipedia: Vasa (ship); vasamuseet.se]
- Maiden voyage 10 August 1628. Sailed about 1,300 m, heeled over, water came in through the open gun ports, sank within minutes. About 30 people died. [Wikipedia; Vasa Museum]
- Before departure a stability test was run at the quay: 30 men ran across the deck; it rolled so badly the test was stopped after three runs. Admiral Fleming reportedly wished the king were at home. The voyage went ahead. [vasamuseet.se audio guide, "8. The inquiry"]
- Inquiry convened by the privy council; no one was punished. [vasamuseet.se]
- Raised April 1961 after over 300 years; purpose-built museum opened 1990; 45M+ visitors since 1961. [Wikipedia; Vasa Museum]
- Cause: top-heavy (extra gun deck, too little ballast). Ballast detail comes from secondary sources: CHECK with the museum.
- UNVERIFIED, do not use unless confirmed: second gust detail, exact crew number, "mud preserved it" specifics.

## Script (~220 words, ~60 s)

**Hook:** Sweden built the most powerful warship in the world. It sailed for less than a mile.

**Setting:** Stockholm, 1628. The king wanted the biggest navy on the Baltic, and the Vasa was his pride. Sixty-four cannons, two gun decks, and enough gold paint to blind the enemy.

**Action:** Thousands watched from the shore. The sails went up. A breeze came in. The ship leaned a little. Then it leaned a lot.

**Snap:** Water poured in through the open gun ports. The Vasa went straight to the bottom.

**Deadpan double:** Luckily, nobody had to explain it to the enemy. Unluckily, they had to explain it to the king.

**Explanation:** Here is the problem. Tall and heavy up top, light down low. It is the same reason you should never stand up in a canoe. They had even tested it at the dock: thirty men ran across the deck, and the ship rolled so badly they stopped. Nobody wanted to tell the king.

**Consequence:** The inquiry found no one to blame. Everyone responsible had a very good reason.

**Callback twist:** The Vasa sat in the mud for three hundred and thirty-three years. Then it was lifted out, and it turned out to be almost perfect. It sailed for less than a mile, and people have been coming to see it ever since.
