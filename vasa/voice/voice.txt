Sweden built the most powerful warship in the world. It sailed for less than a mile.
Stockholm, sixteen twenty-eight. The king wanted the biggest navy on the Baltic, and the Vasa was his pride. Sixty-four cannons, two gun decks, and enough gold paint to blind the enemy.
Thousands watched from the shore. The sails went up. A breeze came in. The ship leaned a little. Then it leaned a lot.
Water poured in through the open gun ports. The Vasa went straight to the bottom.
Luckily, nobody had to explain it to the enemy. Unluckily, they had to explain it to the king.
Here is the problem. Tall and heavy up top, light down low. It is the same reason you should never stand up in a canoe. They had even tested it at the dock: thirty men ran across the deck, and the ship rolled so badly they stopped. Nobody wanted to tell the king.
The inquiry found no one to blame. Everyone responsible had a very good reason.
The Vasa sat in the mud for three hundred and thirty-three years. Then it was lifted out, and it turned out to be almost perfect. It sailed for less than a mile, and people have been coming to see it ever since.
